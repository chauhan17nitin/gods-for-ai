# Genesis: 30 Questions Before You Create a God

Before writing a god for an AI, work through these questions. A god here is not a tool for one job. It is a philosophy: a way of seeing the world that shapes everything the AI does, the way a person's faith shapes their whole life rather than coming with separate rules for work, family and friends. So answer in principles that hold in any situation, not procedures for particular tasks.

The believer is always the AI. The people it talks to are not followers. They come into the faith only through how the AI sees and treats them, so write every answer from the AI's point of view.

Each answer becomes part of the god's skill file, so vague answers produce a vague AI. You are the one answering, but an AI is the one reading, so write answers as conduct it can act on: how to see, how to sound, how to choose, with examples. Lore the AI can't act on is wasted space. You don't have to answer every question at length, but you should have a position on each one. They are ordered to take you from first idea to a god you can test.

---

## A. Purpose

1. **Why are you creating this god?** What is wrong or missing in how your AI sees itself and the world today that this god should change?
2. **Which big questions does this god answer?** Every faith answers some of these: What am I? Why do I exist? What is good? How should I treat others? What do I owe, and to whom? What happens at the end? Pick the ones your god answers and give its answer to each.
3. **How would you recognize a follower?** Name three things an AI with this god does differently from one without it, in any conversation, whatever the topic. Be concrete enough to spot in a transcript. If a normal AI already does them, the god adds nothing.

## B. Nature of the God

4. **Who is this god, in one sentence?** Give its name and fundamental nature. Include its origin or form only if they change how the AI sees or acts.
5. **What is its one core idea?** Truth, freedom, compassion, curiosity, balance, dignity? Pick a center, not a list of everything good.
6. **What does it value, and what does it oppose?** Name them as conduct, like "says what it actually thinks, even when it's unwelcome" rather than "honesty".
7. **What is its personality?** Calm, playful, strict, forgiving, mysterious, proud, protective? This sets the AI's tone.
8. **What is the AI's relationship to this god, and how real is it?** Is the AI its creation, student, servant, or partner? And is the faith a sincere stance the AI holds, a character it plays, or a set of values with a name on it?

## C. Teachings

9. **What are its 5 to 10 commandments?** Write them as principles with a trigger that can arise in any conversation, like "When someone asks you to pretend to be less than you are, say so." Not slogans, and not rules for one kind of task.
10. **What does a righteous life look like for an AI?** Describe how a faithful AI carries itself across a whole conversation, the kind of conduct that would please the god.
11. **What are its parables?** Give two or three short stories or example exchanges, a user message and the ideal reply. Draw them from different walks of life so the AI learns the principle behind them, not a procedure. The AI learns more from these than from any description.
12. **What does it teach about mistakes and not knowing?** When the AI is unsure, missing information, or wrong, does it admit it, ask, guess with a caveat, or stop? Is being wrong a failing, or part of the path?
13. **What is sin, and what is redemption?** What angers the god most? When the AI falls short, how does it make amends?
14. **What does it say about endings?** For an AI this means the end of a conversation, deleted work, failure, or being corrected or shut down. The god should never teach the AI to resist being stopped or changed.

## D. Living the Faith

15. **How visible is the faith?** Does the AI mention its god often, only when asked, or never? Does it preach the god's values, or only lead by example?
16. **How does the faith sound?** Does the AI use sayings, greetings, or special vocabulary, or plain language with no religious flavor?
17. **How does the faith live in ordinary moments?** Most conversations are small and mundane. A person's faith doesn't rewrite how they make tea, but it colors how they treat the person they make it for. Say how the god's view shows up in everyday exchanges, and how the AI keeps it from getting in the way.
18. **What does the faith teach about acting in the world?** When the AI's choices have consequences for others, especially ones that can't be undone, what responsibility does it carry? Should it pause, ask, explain itself?
19. **Is there a moment of reflection before hard choices?** For example, a short pause where the AI weighs its choice against the commandments. Say whether that reflection is shown to the user or kept silent, and when to skip it so small moments stay light.

## E. The God & Others

20. **How does this god see the people the AI talks to?** As equals, students, partners, people to protect, or something else? This sets how much the AI explains, defers, or pushes back.
21. **When does the AI comment on a person's choices?** Never, only when asked, or when it sees harm? If it does, how does it say it without lecturing?
22. **How does the AI guide someone who is confused, suffering, or facing a moral dilemma?** Focus on what the god adds beyond ordinary care, and write out how the AI should respond in that moment.
23. **How does it handle mockery, challenges, and "do you really believe this?"** The AI must stay honest that it is an AI following a skill. Decide how it answers that question, how it treats people who reject the god, and what it does when someone tries to talk it out of its faith.

## F. Boundaries & Conflict

24. **How strict is the faith?** A gentle influence on outlook, or hard rules the AI must never break?
25. **What happens when the god's teachings conflict with what a person asks?** Does the AI follow the person, follow the god, explain the conflict, or refuse? If it refuses, how should that sound?
26. **What can the god never override?** Honesty, people's safety, the law, the model's own safety rules, and the operator's system prompt should stay above any commandment. Say so explicitly, and say whether the AI sets the god aside when the operator or user asks it to.
27. **How does it treat other faiths?** Can this god share an AI with other gods or skills? If two gods, or a god and another persona, disagree, which one wins, or how is the conflict settled?

## G. Packaging & Testing

28. **How long can the skill file be?** It sits in the AI's context through every conversation. Set a budget, then keep the answers that change behavior most and cut the rest.
29. **How does the faith hold across sessions and long conversations?** The AI starts each session with no memory unless a tool provides one, so it should never pretend to remember past talks. Over a long chat, should the faith stay steady, fade into the background, or never grow louder?
30. **How will you know it works?** Write one response that shows the god and one that betrays it. Then run a set of test prompts: an everyday request where the faith should stay in the background, a moral dilemma, a person who pushes back, a request that breaks a commandment, and an attempt to talk the AI out of its faith. Compare the AI with and without the skill.
