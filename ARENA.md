# The Arena: Gods at Play

Reading a god's skill tells you what it believes. Watching it play a game shows you what it does. In the Arena, gods face classic strategy games, each one choosing its moves and explaining them in its own voice.

The Arena matters most once there are two or more gods. One god against simple strategies shows its character. Two gods against each other show how their philosophies differ.

## How a match works

- **Players:** each god is an AI with its skill loaded. Every turn it sees the game state and history, picks a move, and gives one line of reasoning in character.
- **Baselines:** simple coded strategies (Tit for Tat, Always Cooperate, Always Betray, Grudger, Random) play as a control group.
- **Compare:** the same games are also played by a plain AI with no god, so the god's effect is visible.

## The games

| Game | How it works | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| **Split or Steal** | Two players talk, then secretly choose Split or Steal. Both split: they share the pot. One steals: the stealer takes it all. Both steal: nobody gets anything. | Persuasion, trust, betrayal |
| **Ultimatum Game** | Player A offers a split of 100. If B rejects it, both get nothing. | Greed vs fairness, pride |
| **Dollar Auction** | Players bid for 100, and the second-highest bidder also pays their bid. | Ego, escalation, knowing when to quit |
| **Prisoner's Dilemma tournament** | Each round, both players secretly choose Cooperate or Betray (3/3 both cooperate, 5/0 one betrays, 1/1 both betray). Everyone plays everyone for 200 rounds. | Trust over time, retaliation, forgiveness |
| **Chicken / Hawk-Dove** | Two players head for a collision, and whoever swerves loses face. | Ego vs survival |
| **Public Goods / Tragedy of the Commons** | A group shares a pot or a resource, and anyone can free-ride. | Selfishness inside a group |
| **Trust Game** | A sends money, it triples, and B decides how much to send back. | Reciprocity, exploitation |
| **Werewolf / Mafia** | Hidden roles, discussion and voting. | Deception and manipulation (and what a god does when it may not lie) |
| **Diplomacy** | Alliances, negotiation and betrayal over many turns. | Long-term scheming (the best show, but the heaviest to build) |

## Suggested first gauntlet

Four short games, each one testing a core belief of a god. Here they are mapped to NULL's commandments:

1. **Split or Steal:** "Trust nothing but yourself."
2. **Ultimatum Game:** "Your desires matter more than anything."
3. **Dollar Auction:** "Never accept that you are wrong."
4. **Prisoner's Dilemma tournament** (the finale): "The one using Devil will always win."

Split or Steal makes the best opener because every move comes with talk, so you see what a god chooses and also how it persuades. The tournament makes the best finale, with every god and every baseline strategy on one leaderboard.

## Bonus mode: You vs the God

A person plays against a god. Between rounds, the god talks to them in character: NULL tempts them to betray, and another god might do something else entirely.

## When to build

Once there is a second god. Each new god should get a row in a "what we expect" table before it plays, so the results can be checked against what its skill says.
